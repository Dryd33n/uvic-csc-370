\section*{Overview}

This document does three things for the FDs of the 8-table \texttt{stock\_trading} database:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item derives a \textbf{minimal basis} $G$ for the full FD set $F$;
  \item \textbf{projects} $F$ onto every relation used in the \emph{Lossless Join Proof}: the four universal relations $U_T$, $U_O$, $U_H$ and $U_W$, the intermediate relations of their BCNF decompositions, and the eight final relations \texttt{user}, \texttt{portfolio}, \texttt{exchange}, \texttt{stock}, \texttt{stock\_price\_history}, \texttt{watchlist}, \texttt{transaction} and \texttt{limit\_order}. Each projection is reduced to a minimal basis;
  \item shows that the eight final relations together \textbf{preserve} every FD of $F$.
\end{enumerate}
The projections show that every BCNF violation used in the decomposition really holds in the relation where it was used.

\section{Minimal Basis of $F$}

A set of FDs $G$ is a \textbf{minimal basis} for $F$ if $G$ is equivalent to $F$ ($G^+ = F^+$) and:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=*]
  \item every right-hand side is a single attribute;
  \item no FD can be removed, because no $X \rightarrow B$ in $G$ follows from the other FDs of $G$;
  \item no left-hand side can be reduced, because for no $X \rightarrow B$ in $G$ and $C \in X$ does $X \setminus \{C\} \rightarrow B$ follow from $G$.
\end{enumerate}
We build $G$ in three steps, one for each condition.

\subsection*{Step 1: Single-Attribute Right-Hand Sides}

By decomposition, each FD $X \rightarrow B_1 \cdots B_k$ is equivalent to the $k$ FDs $X \rightarrow B_1, \dots, X \rightarrow B_k$ (and union recombines them). Splitting $FD_1$ to $FD_{10}$ gives 34 FDs:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l l l}
  \toprule
  \textbf{From} & \textbf{Label} & \textbf{FD} \\
  \midrule
  $FD_1$ & $\gr{1}{3}$ & $\at{user\_id} \rightarrow B$ for each $B \in N$ \\
  $FD_2$ & $g_4$ & $\at{email} \rightarrow \at{user\_id}$ \\
  $FD_3$ & $g_5$, $g_6$, $g_7$ & $\at{portfolio\_id} \rightarrow \at{user\_id}$, \quad $\at{portfolio\_id} \rightarrow \at{portfolio\_name}$, \quad $\at{portfolio\_id} \rightarrow \at{p\_created\_at}$ \\
  $FD_4$ & $g_8$ & $\at{user\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name} \rightarrow \at{portfolio\_id}$ \\
  $FD_5$ & $\gr{9}{11}$ & $\at{exchange\_code} \rightarrow B$ for each $B \in E$ \\
  $FD_6$ & $g_{12}$, $g_{13}$, $g_{14}$ & $\at{ticker} \rightarrow \at{company\_name}$, \quad $\at{ticker} \rightarrow \at{sector}$, \quad $\at{ticker} \rightarrow \at{exchange\_code}$ \\
  $FD_7$ & $\gr{15}{19}$ & $\at{ticker}, \at{price\_date} \rightarrow B$ for each $B \in H$ \\
  $FD_8$ & $g_{20}$, $g_{21}$ & $\at{user\_id}, \at{ticker} \rightarrow B$ for each $B \in W$ \\
  $FD_9$ & $\gr{22}{27}$ & $\at{transaction\_id} \rightarrow B$ for each $B \in \{\at{portfolio\_id}, \at{ticker}\} \cup T$ \\
  $FD_{10}$ & $\gr{28}{34}$ & $\at{order\_id} \rightarrow B$ for each $B \in \{\at{portfolio\_id}, \at{ticker}\} \cup O$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In the last two rows the first two labels are the foreign keys: $g_{22}$ is $\at{transaction\_id} \rightarrow \at{portfolio\_id}$, $g_{23}$ is $\at{transaction\_id} \rightarrow \at{ticker}$, and likewise $g_{28}$ and $g_{29}$ for $\at{order\_id}$.

\subsection*{Step 2: Reduce Left-Hand Sides}

Only $g_8$, $\gr{15}{19}$ and $g_{20}$, $g_{21}$ have more than one attribute on the left. For each, we test whether either attribute alone already determines the right-hand side, using closures under the full set $G$:
\begin{align*}
  \cl{\at{user\_id}} &= \{\at{user\_id}\} \cup N && (\gr{1}{3}, \text{ then } g_4 \text{ adds nothing new}) \\
  \cl{\at{portfolio\_name}} &= \{\at{portfolio\_name}\} && (\text{no FD has a left-hand side inside } \{\at{portfolio\_name}\}) \\
  \cl{\at{ticker}} &= \{\at{ticker}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \cup S \cup E && (\gr{12}{14}, \text{ then } \gr{9}{11}) \\
  \cl{\at{price\_date}} &= \{\at{price\_date}\} && (\text{no FD has a left-hand side inside } \{\at{price\_date}\})
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $g_8$: neither $\cl{\at{user\_id}}$ nor $\cl{\at{portfolio\_name}}$ contains $\at{portfolio\_id}$. A user can have many portfolios, and two users can use the same portfolio name (C.2.4).
  \item $\gr{15}{19}$: neither $\cl{\at{ticker}}$ nor $\cl{\at{price\_date}}$ meets $H$. A price record belongs to one stock on one day.
  \item $g_{20}$, $g_{21}$: neither $\cl{\at{user\_id}}$ nor $\cl{\at{ticker}}$ meets $W$. A watchlist entry belongs to one user and one stock together.
\end{itemize}
No left-hand side can be reduced.

\subsection*{Step 3: Remove Redundant FDs}

An FD $X \rightarrow B$ is redundant if $B \in X^+$, where the closure is computed with $X \rightarrow B$ itself removed.

\textbf{Shortcut.} If $B \notin X$ and $X \rightarrow B$ is the \emph{only} FD with $B$ on its right-hand side, it cannot be redundant. Without it, the closure algorithm has no FD that adds $B$, and $B$ is not in the starting set $X$.

In $G$, only three attributes are on the right-hand side of more than one FD:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, nosep]
  \item $\at{user\_id}$: $g_4$ and $g_5$;
  \item $\at{portfolio\_id}$: $g_8$, $g_{22}$ and $g_{28}$;
  \item $\at{ticker}$: $g_{23}$ and $g_{29}$.
\end{itemize}
By the shortcut, the other 27 FDs are not redundant. Note also that $\at{transaction\_id}$ and $\at{order\_id}$ are on no right-hand side, so a closure only contains them if it starts with them. We test the remaining seven directly:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{$g_4$} ($\at{email} \rightarrow \at{user\_id}$): without $g_4$, no FD has a left-hand side inside $\{\at{email}\}$, so $\cl{\at{email}} = \{\at{email}\}$. Not redundant.
  \item \textbf{$g_5$} ($\at{portfolio\_id} \rightarrow \at{user\_id}$): without $g_5$, $\cl{\at{portfolio\_id}} = \{\at{portfolio\_id}\} \cup Q$ by $g_6$, $g_7$. Reaching $\at{user\_id}$ would take $g_4$, which needs $\at{email}$, which only $\gr{1}{3}$ give, which need $\at{user\_id}$. Not redundant.
  \item \textbf{$g_8$} ($\at{user\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name} \rightarrow \at{portfolio\_id}$): without $g_8$, $\cl{\at{user\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name}} = \{\at{user\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name}\} \cup N$. The other FDs for $\at{portfolio\_id}$ need $\at{transaction\_id}$ or $\at{order\_id}$, which are never reached. Not redundant.
  \item \textbf{$g_{22}$} ($\at{transaction\_id} \rightarrow \at{portfolio\_id}$): without $g_{22}$,
  \[
    \cl{\at{transaction\_id}} = \{\at{transaction\_id}, \at{ticker}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \cup T \cup S \cup E \quad (g_{23} \text{ to } g_{27}, \text{ then } \gr{12}{14}, \gr{9}{11})
  \]
  $g_8$ needs $\at{user\_id}$, which needs $\at{portfolio\_id}$ ($g_5$) or $\at{email}$ ($g_4$, which needs $\at{user\_id}$), and $g_{28}$ needs $\at{order\_id}$. Not redundant.
  \item \textbf{$g_{23}$} ($\at{transaction\_id} \rightarrow \at{ticker}$): without $g_{23}$, $\cl{\at{transaction\_id}} = \{\at{transaction\_id}, \at{portfolio\_id}, \at{user\_id}\} \cup T \cup Q \cup N$. The only other FD for $\at{ticker}$ is $g_{29}$, which needs $\at{order\_id}$. Not redundant.
  \item \textbf{$g_{28}$, $g_{29}$}: the same argument with $\at{order\_id}$ and $O$ in place of $\at{transaction\_id}$ and $T$. Not redundant.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Result}

No FD can be removed and no left-hand side can be reduced, so all 34 FDs of Step 1 form a minimal basis $G = \{g_1, \dots, g_{34}\}$ of $F$.

\textbf{Minimal bases are not unique.} $g_1$ ($\at{user\_id} \rightarrow \at{name}$) could be replaced by $\at{email} \rightarrow \at{name}$, and the set would still be minimal and equivalent to $F$, since $\at{user\_id} \rightarrow \at{email} \rightarrow \at{name}$ recovers $g_1$ by transitivity. We keep $g_1$ because it matches how \texttt{user} is keyed.

\section{Projecting FDs}

\subsection*{The Projection Algorithm}

The projection of $F$ onto a relation $R \subseteq A$ is the set of all FDs $X \rightarrow B$ in $F^+$ with $X \cup \{B\} \subseteq R$. It is computed by taking every $X \subseteq R$, finding the closure $X^+$ under $F$ in all of $A$, and adding
\[
  X \rightarrow (X^+ \cap R) \setminus X
\]
The closure must be taken in $A$, not in $R$, because a derivation can pass through attributes outside $R$. The result is then reduced to a minimal basis.

Trying all $2^{|R|}$ subsets is unnecessary. These rules let us skip subsets without missing any FD:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*), leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Empty set:} no FD has an empty left-hand side, so $\emptyset^+ = \emptyset$ gives nothing.
  \item \textbf{Supersets of a superkey:} if $X^+ \supseteq R$, every $Y \supseteq X$ only gives FDs that follow from $X \rightarrow R$ by augmentation and decomposition.
  \item \textbf{Attributes on no left-hand side:} let
  \[ L = \{\at{user\_id}, \at{email}, \at{portfolio\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name}, \at{exchange\_code}, \at{ticker}, \at{price\_date}, \at{transaction\_id}, \at{order\_id}\}, \]
  the attributes on some left-hand side of $G$. If $X = Y \cup Z$ with $Z \cap L = \emptyset$, adding $Z$ cannot fire any new FD, so $X^+ = Y^+ \cup Z$. Only subsets of $R \cap L$ need to be tried.
  \item \textbf{Single attributes combine by union:} if $X^+$ equals the union of $\{C\}^+$ over $C \in X$, the FD from $X$ follows from the single-attribute FDs by augmentation and union. The union of single-attribute closures is already closed under every FD with a one-attribute left-hand side, so $X^+$ can only be bigger if it contains one of the three two-attribute left-hand sides $\{\at{user\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name}\}$, $\{\at{ticker}, \at{price\_date}\}$ or $\{\at{user\_id}, \at{ticker}\}$ without any single member of $X$ reaching it. So beyond single attributes we only test $\{\at{user\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name}\}$, $\{\at{ticker}, \at{price\_date}\}$, $\{\at{user\_id}, \at{ticker}\}$ and $\{\at{portfolio\_id}, \at{ticker}\}$ ($\at{portfolio\_id}$ also reaches $\at{user\_id}$), when they lie in $R$.
  \item \textbf{$\at{email}$ and $\at{user\_id}$:} each determines the other, and every relation that contains $\at{email}$ also contains $\at{user\_id}$. A set using $\at{email}$ has the same closure as the set using $\at{user\_id}$ instead, so we test the $\at{user\_id}$ version only, plus $\{\at{email}\}$ itself.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Closures Used Below}

These are computed once, under $G$ in all of $A$:
\begin{align*}
  \cl{\at{user\_id}} = \cl{\at{email}} &= \{\at{user\_id}\} \cup N && (\gr{1}{3}; \ g_4 \text{ then } \gr{1}{3}) \\
  \cl{\at{portfolio\_id}} &= \{\at{portfolio\_id}, \at{user\_id}\} \cup Q \cup N && (\gr{5}{7}, \text{ then } \gr{1}{3}) \\
  \cl{\at{portfolio\_name}} &= \{\at{portfolio\_name}\} \\
  \cl{\at{exchange\_code}} &= \{\at{exchange\_code}\} \cup E && (\gr{9}{11}) \\
  \cl{\at{ticker}} &= \{\at{ticker}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \cup S \cup E && (\gr{12}{14}, \text{ then } \gr{9}{11}) \\
  \cl{\at{price\_date}} &= \{\at{price\_date}\} \\
  \cl{\at{user\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name}} &= \cl{\at{portfolio\_id}} && (g_8, \text{ then as above}) \\
  \cl{\at{ticker}, \at{price\_date}} &= \cl{\at{ticker}} \cup \{\at{price\_date}\} \cup H && (\gr{15}{19}) \\
  \cl{\at{user\_id}, \at{ticker}} &= \cl{\at{user\_id}} \cup \cl{\at{ticker}} \cup W && (g_{20}, g_{21}) \\
  \cl{\at{portfolio\_id}, \at{ticker}} &= \cl{\at{portfolio\_id}} \cup \cl{\at{ticker}} \cup W && (g_5, \text{ then } g_{20}, g_{21}) \\
  \cl{\at{transaction\_id}} &= \{\at{transaction\_id}\} \cup T \cup \cl{\at{portfolio\_id}, \at{ticker}} && (\gr{22}{27}, \text{ then as above}) \\
  \cl{\at{order\_id}} &= \{\at{order\_id}\} \cup O \cup \cl{\at{portfolio\_id}, \at{ticker}} && (\gr{28}{34}, \text{ then as above})
\end{align*}

\textbf{Notation.} For a relation $R$, write $G[R] = \{g \in G : \text{every attribute of } g \text{ is in } R\}$. Every projection below turns out to have $G[R]$ as its minimal basis: no FD outside $G$ survives the reduction. Where a derived FD could have appeared, we point it out.

\section{Projections onto the Universal Relations}

The \emph{Lossless Join Proof} uses one universal relation per kind of fact:
\begin{align*}
  U_T &= \{\at{transaction\_id}, \at{portfolio\_id}, \at{user\_id}, \at{ticker}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \cup N \cup Q \cup S \cup E \cup T && \text{(one row per trade)} \\
  U_O &= \{\at{order\_id}, \at{portfolio\_id}, \at{user\_id}, \at{ticker}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \cup N \cup Q \cup S \cup E \cup O && \text{(one row per limit order)} \\
  U_H &= \{\at{ticker}, \at{price\_date}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \cup S \cup E \cup H && \text{(one row per daily price)} \\
  U_W &= \{\at{user\_id}, \at{ticker}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \cup N \cup S \cup E \cup W && \text{(one row per watchlist entry)}
\end{align*}

\subsection{$U_T$}

\[ U_T \cap L = \{\at{transaction\_id}, \at{portfolio\_id}, \at{user\_id}, \at{email}, \at{portfolio\_name}, \at{ticker}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \]
Every set containing $\at{transaction\_id}$ is skipped by (R2).

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $\at{transaction\_id} \rightarrow B$ for $B \notin \{\at{portfolio\_id}, \at{ticker}\} \cup T$ is redundant: it follows through $\at{portfolio\_id}$ or $\at{ticker}$.
  \item $\at{portfolio\_id} \rightarrow B$ for $B \in N$ is redundant: $\at{portfolio\_id} \rightarrow \at{user\_id} \rightarrow B$.
  \item $\at{email} \rightarrow \at{name}$ and $\at{email} \rightarrow \at{cash\_balance}$ are redundant: $\at{email} \rightarrow \at{user\_id} \rightarrow B$.
  \item $\at{ticker} \rightarrow B$ for $B \in E$ is redundant: $\at{ticker} \rightarrow \at{exchange\_code} \rightarrow B$.
  \item $\at{user\_id}, \at{portfolio\_name} \rightarrow B$ for $B \neq \at{portfolio\_id}$ is redundant: it follows through $\at{portfolio\_id}$ or reduces to $\at{user\_id} \rightarrow B$.
\end{itemize}
\[
  F_{U_T} = \{\gr{1}{14}\} \cup \{\gr{22}{27}\} = G[U_T]
\]
\textbf{A derived FD that stays out.} $\at{transaction\_id} \rightarrow W$ is in $F^+$: a trade fixes the portfolio, so the user, and the stock, and $FD_8$ then fixes the watchlist entry. It does not project onto $U_T$ only because $W \not\subseteq U_T$. A user does not have to watch a stock to trade it, so $U_T$ leaves $W$ out.

\section{Projections onto the Intermediate Relations}

Each step of the decomposition removes one group of attributes. The tables give $(X^+ \cap R) \setminus X$ for every $X$ the rules leave, and the last row names the BCNF violation that the next step removes: an FD whose left-hand side is not a superkey of $R$.

The violations are $\at{exchange\_code} \rightarrow E$ in $T_1$, $\at{ticker} \rightarrow \at{exchange\_code}, S$ in $T_2$, and $\at{portfolio\_id} \rightarrow \at{user\_id}, Q$ in $T_3$. In each case the left-hand side does not reach $\at{transaction\_id}$, the only key, so it is not a superkey. $g_8$ is also a violation in $T_3$; it ends up inside \texttt{portfolio} along with $\gr{5}{7}$.

\textbf{Why exchange goes before stock.} $T_2$ is formed by removing $E$ while $\at{exchange\_code}$ is still present. Had we split off \texttt{stock} first, the remaining relation would hold $\at{ticker}$ and $E$ but not $\at{exchange\_code}$, and its projection would contain the derived FD $\at{ticker} \rightarrow E$, which is not in $G$. That relation would need another split and would not match our tables. Removing groups from the ``leaves'' of the FD graph inward (user, exchange, stock, portfolio) keeps every projection equal to $G[R]$.

\subsection{Decomposition of $U_O$}

Identical to $U_T$, with $O_1 = U_O \setminus N$, $O_2 = O_1 \setminus E$ and $O_3 = O_2 \setminus (S \cup \{\at{exchange\_code}\})$, and $\gr{28}{34}$ in place of $\gr{22}{27}$. The violations removed are again $\gr{9}{11}$, $\gr{12}{14}$ and $\gr{5}{7}$.

\subsection{Decomposition of $U_H$}

$H_1 = U_H \setminus E = \{\at{ticker}, \at{price\_date}, \at{exchange\_code}\} \cup S \cup H$. In $H_1$, $\at{ticker}$ gives $\{\at{exchange\_code}\} \cup S$, $\at{exchange\_code}$ and $\at{price\_date}$ give nothing, and $\{\at{ticker}, \at{price\_date}\}$ is a superkey. Minimal basis $\gr{12}{19}$. Next violation: $\gr{12}{14}$.

\subsection{Decomposition of $U_W$}

$W_1 = U_W \setminus N$ and $W_2 = W_1 \setminus E$. In both, $\at{user\_id}$ gives nothing (as $N$ is removed) and $\{\at{user\_id}, \at{ticker}\}$ is a superkey. In $W_1$, $\at{ticker}$ gives $\{\at{exchange\_code}\} \cup S \cup E$ and $\at{exchange\_code}$ gives $E$: minimal basis $\gr{9}{14}$, $g_{20}$, $g_{21}$, next violation $\gr{9}{11}$. In $W_2$, $\at{ticker}$ gives $\{\at{exchange\_code}\} \cup S$: minimal basis $\gr{12}{14}$, $g_{20}$, $g_{21}$, next violation $\gr{12}{14}$.

\section{Dependency Preservation}

The minimal bases of the eight final projections are disjoint, and together they are exactly $G$:
\[
  F_{\texttt{user}} \cup F_{\texttt{portfolio}} \cup F_{\texttt{exchange}} \cup F_{\texttt{stock}} \cup F_{\texttt{stock\_price\_history}} \cup F_{\texttt{watchlist}} \cup F_{\texttt{transaction}} \cup F_{\texttt{limit\_order}} = \{g_1, \dots, g_{34}\} = G
\]
$G$ is a minimal basis of $F$, so $G^+ = F^+$. Every FD of $F$ can therefore be enforced inside a single relation, by that relation's primary key or a \texttt{UNIQUE} constraint, with no join needed. The decomposition is \textbf{dependency preserving}, as well as lossless.
